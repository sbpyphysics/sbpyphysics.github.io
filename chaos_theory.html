Anisotropy-Induced Localization in Box Fractal Lattices

This project investigates quantum transport in deterministic box fractal lattices with anisotropic hopping parameters using a tight-binding model. By introducing direction-dependent hopping strengths, the study examines how anisotropy influences the localization and propagation of quantum particles in self-similar fractal geometries. The Hamiltonian of the box fractal is constructed numerically, and its eigenvalue spectrum and eigenstates are analyzed through exact diagonalization. Wave-packet dynamics are simulated to compute the mean square displacement (MSD), allowing the diffusion exponent to be extracted and used to characterize transport regimes ranging from ballistic and diffusive motion to subdiffusive behavior and localization. The effects of varying anisotropy on transport properties are systematically explored, revealing the transition from extended to localized quantum states.

Objectives

  • Construct deterministic box fractal lattices using an iterative algorithm.
  • Investigate the effect of anisotropic hopping on quantum transport.
  • Compute wave-packet evolution and mean square displacement numerically.
  • Determine diffusion exponents and identify localization transitions.

Tools Used

Python, NumPy, SciPy, Matplotlib

Results

The simulations demonstrate that increasing hopping anisotropy significantly suppresses quantum transport in box fractal lattices. The diffusion exponent decreases from values corresponding to extended transport toward subdiffusive and localized regimes, indicating anisotropy-induced localization. These results provide insight into the interplay between fractal geometry and directional disorder, contributing to the understanding of quantum transport in complex self-similar systems.
