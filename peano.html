Quantum Localization and Transport in Peano Fractal Lattices

This project investigates the spectral and transport properties of deterministic Peano fractal lattices within the tight-binding framework. The electronic density of states (DOS) is first analyzed to understand the influence of the fractal geometry and hopping anisotropy on the energy spectrum. Direction-dependent hopping amplitudes are introduced through an anisotropy parameter, allowing a systematic study of how anisotropy modifies the spectral characteristics of the lattice. To explore localization phenomena, Aubry-André-Harper (AAH) quasiperiodic modulation is incorporated into the hopping amplitudes. The interplay between the self-similar fractal geometry, hopping anisotropy, and quasiperiodic modulation gives rise to a reentrant localization transition, where the system alternates between extended and localized transport regimes as the modulation strength is varied. The localization transition is quantified through quantum dynamical simulations by calculating the time evolution of wave packets and their mean square displacement (MSD). The diffusion exponent extracted from the MSD provides a quantitative measure of the transport regime, enabling a clear characterization of extended, subdiffusive, and localized phases.

Objectives

  • Construct a Peano fractal lattice using the tight-binding model.
  • Analyze the density of states (DOS) under varying hopping anisotropy.
  • Introduce Aubry-André-Harper (AAH) hopping modulation.
  • Investigate reentrant localization in quasiperiodic fractal lattices.
  • Characterize quantum transport using mean square displacement (MSD) and diffusion exponents.

Tools Used

Python, NumPy, SciPy, Matplotlib

Results

The study demonstrates that the interplay between fractal geometry, hopping anisotropy, and AAH quasiperiodic modulation leads to rich localization behavior. Quantum dynamical simulations reveal a reentrant localization transition, where the system exhibits multiple transitions between extended and localized states. Mean square displacement analysis and the corresponding diffusion exponents provide a quantitative understanding of transport in quasiperiodic Peano fractal lattices.
