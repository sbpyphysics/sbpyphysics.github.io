Re-entrant Localization Transition in Quasiperiodic Zig-Zag Ladder Networks

This project investigates quantum transport and localization in a quasi-one-dimensional zig-zag ladder within the tight-binding framework. A quasiperiodic Aubry-André-Harper (AAH) modulation is introduced in the next-nearest-neighbor hopping amplitudes to explore the interplay between kinetic energy and quasiperiodic disorder. Unlike conventional localization, the system exhibits a remarkable re-entrant localization-delocalization transition controlled by the slowness exponent of the AAH modulation. The localization properties are characterized through inverse participation ratio (IPR), normalized participation ratio (NPR), finite-size scaling, and multifractal analysis, revealing the presence of single-particle mobility edges (SPME). The transport behavior is further quantified by real-time quantum dynamics using the mean square displacement (MSD), demonstrating the coexistence of ballistic, subdiffusive, and localized transport regimes. In addition, the quasiperiodic modulation gives rise to a Hofstadter-like quantum butterfly spectrum together with robust edge-localized states. Their stability against hopping disorder is investigated, demonstrating the persistence of boundary modes up to a critical disorder strength.

Objectives

  • Model a quasiperiodic zig-zag ladder using the tight-binding Hamiltonian.
  • Investigate re-entrant localization induced by quasiperiodic hopping modulation.
  • Identify single-particle mobility edges using IPR, NPR, and multifractal analysis.
  • Characterize quantum transport through mean square displacement (MSD).
  • Study quantum butterfly spectra and the robustness of edge-localized states.

Tools Used

Python, NumPy, SciPy, Matplotlib

Results

The numerical simulations demonstrate a re-entrant localization-delocalization transition driven by the slowness exponent of the quasiperiodic hopping modulation. The coexistence of extended and localized eigenstates confirms the emergence of single-particle mobility edges, while quantum dynamical simulations verify the transition through the transport exponent extracted from the mean square displacement. The system also exhibits a multifractal quantum butterfly spectrum and robust edge-localized modes that remain stable against moderate hopping disorder, providing new insight into localization phenomena in quasiperiodic low-dimensional lattices.
